# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three audiences arrive at the public site, in rough priority order:

1. **Prospective clients** — people considering a commission (weddings, portraits, commercial). They need to quickly gauge the quality and range of the work, build trust, and find an easy way to get in touch.
2. **Editors & galleries** — publications, curators, and agencies evaluating the work for features or representation. They scan for a distinct point of view and a coherent body of work.
3. **Admirers & followers** — a general audience enjoying and sharing the photography, and reading the journal/blog.

Context: mostly desktop and mobile browsing, often referred from social or word of mouth. The visit is short and visual — the work has to land in seconds.

## Product Purpose

An editorial, gallery-first portfolio for the photographer Piyush Gogawale. It exists to present the photography at its best, establish a recognizable editorial identity, and convert interest into contact. Success looks like: a visitor immediately grasps the calibre and feel of the work, explores a gallery or two, and either reaches out (clients/editors) or returns and shares (admirers). The site is self-serve via a CMS, so the photographer keeps it current without developers.

## Brand Personality

Editorial and refined — magazine-grade and considered, near-serif elegance (Fraunces) paired with clean Inter. Warmth comes from the cream + sunset palette and the photography itself, never from decoration. Voice is quiet and confident: the work speaks, the chrome stays out of the way. Three words: **editorial, warm, restrained.**

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / template** — cookie-cutter startup landing pages, identical icon-heading-text card grids, hero-metric blocks, tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section.
- **Cluttered photo-proofing sites** — dense thumbnail walls, watermarks, and stocky UI (SmugMug/Pixieset defaults).
- **Dark moody-photographer cliché** — all-black backgrounds, neon accents, heavy drop shadows; the default "edgy photographer" look.
- **Loud / overdesigned** — competing animations, gradients everywhere, chrome that distracts from the images.

## Design Principles

- **The photograph is the hero.** Chrome stays quiet and flat; depth and drama come from the imagery, not the interface. Reserve elevation for the lightbox and framed images only.
- **Editorial restraint over decoration.** Refined, magazine-grade composition. Negative space, considered type, and one deliberate accent — never effects for their own sake.
- **Warmth without noise.** The sunset palette and the signature stripe band carry emotion and identity; they are accents, not the whole surface. Orange is reserved for the single primary action per view.
- **One clear path to contact.** Every audience can reach out effortlessly; the Contact CTA is the consistent primary action, present without shouting.
- **Always current, self-serve.** The photographer owns the content. New galleries, posts, and pages appear without a developer, so the design must hold up with real, changing content (varied photo counts, long titles, empty states).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (mind muted text on cream surfaces), large text ≥3:1. Full keyboard operability for the carousel, gallery lightbox, and contact form; visible focus states. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` with crossfade/instant alternatives for the carousel and any reveal motion. Meaningful alt text on every image (the CMS requires it on upload). Touch targets ≥44px. No information conveyed by color alone.
